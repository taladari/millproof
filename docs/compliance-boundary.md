# The boundary

Millproof is run from Israel by someone who is not a licensed US customs broker and cannot
become one. Under 19 CFR 111.11 an individual broker license requires US citizenship. This
document is the line the product and every piece of copy stay behind. It is a gate, not a
preference.

## The regulation

19 CFR 111.1 defines customs business as activities involving transactions with CBP concerning
entry and admissibility, classification and valuation, payment of duties, and refunds. It then
extends:

> "'Customs business' also includes the preparation, and activities relating to the
> preparation, of documents in any format and the electronic transmission of documents and
> parts of documents intended to be filed with CBP in furtherance of any other customs business
> activity, **whether or not signed or filed by the preparer**."

And it carves out:

> "'Customs business' does not include the mere electronic transmission of data received for
> transmission to CBP and does not include a **corporate compliance activity**."

19 CFR 111.2 confirms an importer transacting customs business solely on its own account needs
no license, and neither do its employees acting for it.

## What that means in practice

**Millproof may:**

- Store and version an importer's own records: metal weight, metal value, country of melt and
  pour or smelt and cast, mill certificates, supplier declarations, BOMs.
- Run supplier data collection on the importer's behalf as their compliance tooling.
- Compute arithmetic the importer defines - aggregate weights, the 15% threshold test - and
  show its inputs.
- Export data in open formats that the importer or its licensed broker chooses to use.
- Publish general, sourced explanations of published CBP requirements, with the CSMS number.

**Millproof may not:**

- Determine or recommend an HTS classification for a customer's merchandise.
- Prepare, populate, review or transmit an entry, entry summary, PSC, protest or refund claim.
- Advise on valuation, admissibility, duty treatment, or how to structure an import.
- Represent a customer before CBP in any capacity.
- Describe the gap check as a classification tool, an audit, or a compliance opinion.

## Copy rules

Every customer-facing sentence passes these:

1. Say "indicative" and "verify with your broker" wherever scope is shown. The landing page does
   this in the results footnote and in the FAQ.
2. Never write "you owe", "you should declare", "this is classified as", or "we will file".
3. Attribute every requirement to its CSMS bulletin or CFR section, with a date. If it cannot
   be cited, it cannot be claimed.
4. The disclaimer block stays on the page, above the fold of the pilot offer, in every version.

## Standing test

If a feature would be useless unless the customer treats our output as the answer they file,
that feature is over the line. Ship the evidence, never the conclusion.
